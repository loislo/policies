SmashRadar Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 November 2026

SmashRadar measures the speed of badminton smashes with the phone's camera and microphone. This policy applies to the SmashRadar apps for iPhone and Android.

Summary

SmashRadar does not collect, send or sell any data. Everything the app records and measures stays on your phone. The app has no account, no advertising and no analytics.

Camera and microphone

  • The camera records the shuttle at 240 frames per second, to measure the speed of the smash.
  • The microphone records the sound of the hit, to find the moment of the smash in the video.
  • The app uses the camera and the microphone only while it is open on the screen. The recordings and the measured speeds are stored on your phone only.

Data on your phone

  • Recordings (video and sound), the measured shots and an app log are stored in the app's own storage on your phone. You can delete them in the app at any time; deleting the app deletes them all.
  • To find which person hit a shot, the app looks at the players in the video on your phone. It does not identify who they are, and nothing about them leaves the phone.
  • On the iPhone, the recordings are excluded from the phone's iCloud backup.

Data that leaves your phone

  • The app itself sends nothing over the internet. (The Android app does not even have the permission to use the internet.)
  • When you choose to save a clip to your photos or to share it, the clip goes to the photo library or the app you choose. What happens to it there is governed by that app.
  • If you test the app through Apple TestFlight or install it from an app store, Apple or Google may collect information such as crash reports and usage statistics under their own privacy policies, and only if you allow it in your phone's settings. SmashRadar does not receive personal data from them.

Children

The app does not collect data from anyone, including children.

Changes

If the app ever starts to send data (for example an online leaderboard), this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the app will ask for your consent where the law requires it.
